# Backfill: AI search, and closing the critic's gaps

## ai-search

Content extractability is not the problem: every AI crawler I tested (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, OAI-SearchBot, CCBot) receives an identical HTTP 200 with the full 90,549-byte server-rendered HTML, and the visible text extracts cleanly at 978 words on /classes/diabetes. The problem is that the site has no machine-readable identity and no third-party corroboration, so AI systems have nothing to resolve or cite. A literal brand search for "myyogaclasses.fit" returned nine results, zero of which were the live site; result #1 was the public GitHub repo, whose description still says "PayPal" and "AU customers", and the generated answer concluded the domain "appears to be a web application framework designed for yoga studios rather than an active yoga class website itself." Meanwhile the site ships 45 FAQ question-answer pairs and three contradictory review counts (1,200+, 312, and 6 actually published) with zero FAQPage, Review, AggregateRating, Service or Offer markup anywhere, and its Course nodes each assert a different Organization sameAs, actively fragmenting the entity across nine pages. I correct four items from the first-pass audit: condition pages have NO FAQPage schema (item 7 is wrong), the pose data is 55 unique poses across 35 groups (not 107 across 36) and is already published as on-page sections, /reviews renders 6 reviews not 12, and a global visitor is shown roughly $10 per session in rupees, not $23-24. My verdict on the pose-library strategy is no: the informational SERP is held by Healthline, WebMD and the American Diabetes Association under YMYL weighting, while the commercial SERP is held by a direct competitor with a plain service page, so the effort belongs on entity and commercial surfaces.
